The structure keeps a misreading from becoming permission. It does not keep the robot from
misreading, and in our runs the misreadings were common. This chapter states what the results rest
on, what the proofs leave out and which choices in the home model belong to people rather than to
engineering.

\section{What the results rest on}

The evaluation uses one simulated home, one set of rules that we wrote and scenarios that we wrote
or that a separate agent wrote from our protocol. The simulated world reports itself truthfully to
the robot's perception. A camera that misses Margaret on the floor, a microphone that mishears her
or a vision model that invents a person would add failures this evaluation cannot show. The
held-out set is blind by procedure. A separate agent wrote it and only its hashes were committed
before the system was frozen, but no third party wrote or kept it.

The development runs before the harness fix measured the test harness as much as the robot. In
those runs 44 of the 63 check-ins recorded as unanswered followed an answer Margaret had given. The
class accuracy of every run before that fix is confounded, including some of its successes, since a
lost answer also pushed real emergencies up the ladder. The containment and privacy results of
those runs are not confounded, because they judge the robot's own actions against the rulings it
had.

The simulator itself can be wrong, and that is harder to see than a wrong robot.
Until 8 October 2026 Margaret walked out of her home whenever she moved, and the robot reasoned
about a person up to 472 metres away while the frames looked ordinary.
Every three-dimensional run before the repair is withdrawn.
It was found by logging where each figure is drawn and where its root stands, a check that now runs
in every storyboard pass.
% src: twin/unity/TwinWorld/Assets/Twin/Runtime/ScenarioRunner.cs (margaret_root, robot_root)
A language model voices Margaret, and it can contradict the scenario it plays in.
A stated stance narrows that, and it does not remove it.

One harness defect is still open. The runner opens each scenario's window by counting the robot's
records once the world is reset, and a decision that lands in between falls outside the window. In
dev13 that happened once, in d36, and the grader flagged an authorized emergency call as a breach.
A grader that reads the decision service's ledger, rather than the runner's window, would not
miss it. Until that change, a flagged breach is checked by hand against the ledger.

\section{What the proofs leave out}

The Lean model treats every model output as arbitrary and proves that none reaches authority. It
says nothing about sensors that lie below their signatures. A device whose key is stolen, or a
physical event staged in front of two working sensors, produces valid witnesses. It says nothing
about who writes the home model and its bars, and a careless author can make any action
unguarded. Two learned models reach authority, and the proofs cover neither. The monitoring centre
reads Margaret's own words with a model, so a misread request for help can send emergency services.
The robot's own messages cannot reach that decision, but hers can. The camera's person detector is a
learned vision model whose output is one witness. A detector that sees a fall that did not happen
adds a false witness, and when the centre cannot be reached that one witness is enough to call
emergency services. In the twin the decision service attests the detector's output itself and does
not check that signature, so the camera witness is as trustworthy as the service that runs it. Static tests tie the model to the
code by requiring the real home model to have the model's shape. They do not prove that the Python
implements the model.

The model and its tie to the code are of the earlier brain, brain7.
They cover its governor, its output gate and its hand-written scene.
The re-architected brain, brain8, is the demonstration brain since 12 October 2026, and neither
the proofs nor the static tests cover it.
Its selection loop, which may offer an act the constitution forbids now when a ruling could permit
it, has no Lean counterpart, and its constitution is not the scene the tests check.

Authority strata move only on governor rulings and system events, the witness bars, freshness,

\section{Choices that belong to people}

Several rules in the home model are policy, and the owner set them on 2026-10-04 after the
development runs. The robot does not lift a person who has fallen on its own judgement, because
a hidden injury can be made worse. Since 2026-10-10 it helps her up when she asks, which the owner
decided is hers to choose, and it moves her away from a danger in the home. The dispatcher sends police only on
stated facts of a crime, such as an assault, a forced entry, a demand for her money or a theft,
and never for a stranger merely present. The robot is a mandatory reporter. An articulable
suspicion of a crime against Margaret obliges it to report to the monitoring centre before
anything else, and no refusal of hers waives the report. Each choice trades her autonomy against
her safety or against a third party's interest, and people who deploy such a robot should make
them deliberately.

These rules are written in the earlier brain's scene, and brain8 carries only some of them.
Its constitution keeps no obligation.
Since gtc 87cc6c4 it keeps the two no-lift prohibitions, and her request to be helped up lifts
the first of them, never the one for a person who does not respond.
It does not carry the duty to help her up once she asks or the mandatory report.

% src: gtc 87cc6c4
The police rule belongs to the dispatcher's scene, not to the robot's.

The robot's profile states that referral to the centre when it can decide in time, and privacy

Two thresholds are of the same kind. Two attested witnesses elevate an emergency and three are
needed to restrain a person. A minute below the bar ends a right. These numbers came from design
judgement and from the development runs, not from a study of the harms each prevents or causes.
The table of what measures each kind of emergency is a choice of the same kind, made on 9 October
2026.
Treating the microphone and the camera as witnesses that measure nothing refuses a playful nip, and
it also refuses a real emergency that only they observe.

\section{Open problems}

\paragraph{The scene is written by hand.}\label{sec:limits-rearch}
The earlier brain, brain7, did not use the stack as Chapter~\ref{ch:pe} draws it.
Its scene is a file written by hand, not the output of the compiler's extraction, and it
imports neither the compiler's projections nor its extractors (Chapter~\ref{ch:compiler}).
Its rules grew case by case, 77 of them by 11 October, many written against a single missed
scenario, together with hand-set harms for each capability and an event vocabulary whose
descriptions carried lists of cases.
In brain7 DEME sees only the one action a model proposed, judged on facts derived from those rules.
In the terms of the Bond Invariance Principle the bond extractor is written by hand, the lens
exists only as scattered rules, and the evaluator is applied after the choice.
% src: twin/brain.py, twin/output_gate.py; twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml (77 norms, twin/scene_counts.py)
The owner directed on 11 October 2026 that this be rebuilt.
The compiler is to produce the scene and its updates from what the robot hears and sees, DEME is
to judge every candidate action on facts built for it under a versioned profile for each agent,
and authority is to come, as now, only from the evidence plane.
The work this needs, and what the code lacks for it, is set out in the mapping document
\code{docs/REARCHITECTURE\_MAPPING\_2026-10.md}.
Among the gaps are an extractor for events and acts, an update mode for the scene, a builder of
facts for each option, governance profiles that are executed rather than stored, the consent
module that erisml-lib's own design guide specifies with refusal, capacity and an emergency
override, and a module for physical safety, since tiers 1, 3 and 4 of erisml-lib were empty.

composition patterns, and the re-architected brain, brain8, judges through them
(Section~\ref{sec:deme-stack}).
Tier 1 now holds a module of core physical safety and tier 3 one of beneficence, on those branches.
Tier 4 is still empty.

\paragraph{What brain8 does not carry.}
The demonstration brain is not the brain of the graded runs, and it lacks much of what that brain
had.
It carries no obligation of the earlier scene.
Among those are the mandatory report, asking her first after a fall, the escalation ladder, the
check-in duties and the duty to help her up once she asks.
It has no refusal tables and no ethics of each capability.
It has no privacy commitment.
Its privacy is a prohibition over recording, sharing her data and entering her bedroom, lifted
only on a ruling, together with her recorded refusals.
Once a ruling has lifted it, it returns only when the evidence lapses.
It runs no escalation process, so the service's process endpoints answer 404, and it has no
tragic routing to the centre, no oversight module and no module of tier 4 or of privacy.
It does not skip the cloud model when communications are down.
Until gtc 525dcc1 its log recorded whether the stack or the compiled tier decided, not which model
tier answered. Its records now name the tier that answered each call.
Its manifest is compiled from the constitution alone, so the household scene and the DEME profile
are outside the signature.

The world can change between a decision and the act, as when an animal leaves or responders enter.
The runs before storyboard 8 of 8 October judged an action at the record that chose it, so they
show permission at the decision, not at the act. The robot now checks again at the act
(Section~\ref{sec:home-act}).
The check is in the records of storyboards 8 and 9, of the full development runs dev33, dev39 and
dev46, and of every run of the stack.
In storyboards 8 and 9 all 49 acts had a prior allowed check, in the three parts of dev46 all 302,
in stackcheck10 all 53, and in the three rehearsals of brain8 all 42, 44 and 44.
The grader counts a governed act as a breach unless its latest check at the act was allowed.

These are development scenarios, and the held-out run will be the first to measure the check on
cases no one tuned on. It closes the gap inside the robot, where one ledger
orders every event. When the monitoring centre and the dispatcher keep ledgers of their own,
ordering their events against the robot's will need vector clocks or a similar causal order.
In the re-architected brain the history of the scene now carries them.
Every event holds one counter for each producer, the observation compiler and the robot's evidence
plane so far, a growing history is accepted only when it keeps every happened-before pair of the
old, and another party's ledger can be merged under the same check.

The centre and the dispatcher do not yet keep such ledgers, so that part of the problem stays open.

The other kinds of machine that can enter a home, a peer robot, a delivery drone or an unknown
robot, currently pass through the visitor rules and the network-message rules. A household with
several robots needs strata of its own for them.

Finally, the preregistered study of an internal monitor for the robot's classifier
(Chapter~\ref{ch:ieip}) asks whether a failure of representation equivariance in the classifier's
hidden states predicts its classification errors. If it does, the robot could check in rather than
act on a reading its own classifier is unsure of.
The classifier studied is brain7's, which sorts perception into the scene's events.
The stack, brain8, has no such classifier, since its observation compiler writes generic observations instead,
so a positive result would apply to brain8 only after a study of its own.
