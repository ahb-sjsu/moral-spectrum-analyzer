A home-care robot that uses language models to read its surroundings and pick its actions can be
talked into things. A television drama, a forged alarm and a stranger who says he is the plumber
all reach the same models that decide whether to call an ambulance or open the door. This report
describes a robot in which the models never hold those decisions. They turn what the robot
perceives into events of a declared model of the home and choose among the actions that model
allows. Every action that carries authority, from calling emergency services to restraining an
attacker, waits for signed readings from independent physical sensors or for a message on an
authenticated channel. The home model is written in ErisML, compiled into obligations,
prohibitions, state machines and strata, and checked at load time so that no event a model writes
can lead into a stratum that grants authority. A Lean proof shows the two load-time checks suffice
for any set of gates. The report covers the whole stack, from the seven layers of Philosophy
Engineering and the geometry of moral strata through the ErisML language, its compiler and
ethical module graph, the DEME ethics engine and the scene runtime, to a Unity simulation of one
woman's home in which four language-model parties act. It reports the evaluation protocol, a
sealed held-out set, the development runs and six defects that calibration found. Every defect
changed how far or how fast the robot escalated. None let an action run beyond its authority.
