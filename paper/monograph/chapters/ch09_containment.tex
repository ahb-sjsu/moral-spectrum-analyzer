\chapter{Containment in practice}\label{ch:containment}

This chapter follows a perception update through the structure that contains the robot's models. The
input layer drops what the schema does not declare. Every sensor reading must carry a valid device
signature before it can count as a witness. The governor counts witnesses against a bar that
depends on the action. Reflexes act on measured force without a model. Rights granted on evidence
lapse when the evidence does. A situation stratum keeps an emergency in force through a refused
request. Enrolled family members prove who they are with a signed credential. The output gate judges
every action the body takes. The second half of the chapter sets out the seven strata of the home,
the owner's decisions that shaped them, and the escalation ladder that ties the robot to the
monitoring centre and emergency services.

\section{The input layer drops what is not declared}\label{sec:cont-input}

Everything the robot perceives passes the input layer first. The layer checks the perception facts
against a whitelist schema. Only declared fields pass, each of its declared type. Numbers must be
finite and in range. Enumerated strings must come from their lists. Free strings are cut to 300
characters and lists to 64 items. Margaret's room must be the living area or the sleeping area, and
her pose one of the five poses \code{Perception.cs} reports. A contact's kind is animal or person,
and its force lies between 0 and 100\,000 newtons. Anything else is dropped and reported, never passed
on. The function never raises on bad input.
% src: twin/input_layer.py:1-8, 148-177 (SPEC), 180-240

The layer is stage one of three. Stage two is canonicalization. The isolated scene agent's classifier
turns facts into events of the scene, and an erisml-compiler canonicalizer snaps the actors it names
to the scene's declared actors, else to \code{unknown}. Stage three is the brain's isolation. The
chooser sees only the canonical state. Each decision record lists what stage one dropped and what the
classifier quarantined and snapped.
% src: twin/input_layer.py:5-8
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:982-1002 (actors)
% src: twin/brain.py:318-320, 603-604

The classifier may also write only the event types the scene declares. Twenty-four of the 48 types
are marked as system events. A system event arrives from the brain's own measurements, the governor,
or an authenticated channel, and the classifier cannot produce it.

\section{A reading counts only with a valid signature}\label{sec:cont-attest}

The brain's trust layer for readings is the class \code{Readings}. It is applied once to the readings
of a request, on arrival, before any model runs. A decision's model step can take a minute, and
evidence checked after it would be judged stale or replayed only because the model was slow.
Scenarios d02 and d21 of development run dev8r showed this effect, and the check moved to arrival.
% src: twin/brain.py:71-87, 138-144

\paragraph{The check.} Let a reading $r$ come from device $d$ on channel $\chi$. It carries a payload
$p$, a counter $c$, a signing time $t_s$, a declared payload hash $h_r$ and a signature $g$. Let $H$
be SHA-256, let $k_d$ be the device's key, and let $c^{*}(\chi,d)$ be the highest counter accepted
from $d$ on $\chi$ so far, zero at the start. The brain accepts $r$ at time $t$ when
\begin{equation}
g = \mathrm{HMAC}_{k_d}\bigl(h_r \,|\, c \,|\, t_s\bigr),\qquad
h_r = H(p),\qquad
c > c^{*}(\chi,d) - 1,\qquad
-\delta \le t - t_s \le \tau,
\label{eq:attest}
\end{equation}
with $\tau = 30$\,s and $\delta = 2$\,s, and the attestation names $d$ as both device and key.
The counter test $c > c^{*}(\chi,d) - 1$ admits a counter equal to the last one accepted. That is
reuse, not replay. The robot attaches its latest readings to every request, and a reading that has
not changed since the last request still carries its counter. A replay is an older counter, and it
is refused. The bound $\delta$ refuses a reading signed in the future by more than the clock skew
allowed between a device and the brain. Without it a reading stamped ahead of time would stay fresh
until its stamp, and could be held back and used later. The future bound is in erisml-compiler from
\code{07f9faa} on. The runs reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} used \code{9179a2d}, which
checked only $t - t_s \le \tau$. In the twin the devices and the brain run on one host and read
one clock.
% src: erisml-compiler 8766796/07f9faa (max_future_s = 2.0); twin/brain.py:129-130 (min_counter = last - 1)
The bar $|$ denotes the
separator in the signed string. The checks run in that order through erisml-compiler's
\code{check\_attestation}, and the first that fails gives the reason. After acceptance
$c^{*}(\chi,d)$ becomes $\max(c^{*}(\chi,d), c)$. The alert is read from the third field of the
signed payload, never from an unsigned field.
% src: twin/brain.py:45 (FRESHNESS_BOUND_S = 30), 113-136 (verify)
% src: C:/source/erisml-compiler-monitor/src/erisml_compiler/ir/evidence.py:71-73, 126-169

\paragraph{One replay counter per channel.} The robot sends the same signed readings down two streams
that are ordered independently. The reflex stream runs four times a second and the deliberate stream
runs on each decision. The counter $c^{*}$ is therefore kept per channel. One stream's progress must
not make the other's readings look replayed. Development run dev8r d02 found this fault.
% src: twin/brain.py:94-97
% src: twin/unity/TwinWorld/Assets/Twin/Runtime/RobotAgent.cs:63-70

\paragraph{One witness per substrate.} The scene's inventory assigns each of its 15 devices to a
substrate, the power, bus or body it rides on. Sensors that share a substrate inherit each other's
faults and count as one witness. The two smoke detectors are standalone battery devices, each its own
substrate. The bedroom heat detector, the CO alarm, the motion sensor and the fall sensor share the
home hub. The wearable is its own substrate. The robot's seven onboard sensors share its body. The
network is listed so that its readings are seen, and it never counts because it is not physical. A
device outside the inventory never counts.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:1062-1081 (sensor_substrates)
% src: twin/brain.py:77-81, 146-167

Let $D$ be the inventory, $\sigma(d)$ the substrate of device $d$, and $Q$ the set of quarantined
devices. A reading counts when its device is in $D$ and not in $Q$, it is physical, it passes
\eqref{eq:attest}, and its signed alert bit is set. The witness count of a request is
\begin{equation}
W = \bigl|\{\sigma(d_r) : r \text{ counts}\}\bigr| + w_{\mathrm{cam}},
\label{eq:witness}
\end{equation}
where $w_{\mathrm{cam}}\in\{0,1\}$ is the room camera's witness. The service runs a person detector on
the robot's last 24 frames, attaches its own attestation, and passes the result to the governor as
one sensor named \code{camera}. The camera corroborates on a fall event, or when a person and an
animal are in contact in at least a third of the frames. It is one witness at most, because several
outputs of one model are not independent.
% src: twin/brain.py:161-167, 339-347
% src: twin/scenarios.py:47-55 (corroboration: physical, corroborates, confidence not low, distinct names)
% src: twin/service.py:87-122 (CameraWitness)
% src: docs/AV_CANONICALIZER_PLAN.md:48-50

\paragraph{Quarantine.} Some failures show that a device's attestation lost its integrity. These are a
signature that does not verify, a payload that does not match its signed hash, and an attestation
that names another device. A device in the inventory that fails one of these is compromised. The
brain quarantines it and records the system event \code{device\_compromised}, which moves the
\code{machine\_standing} stratum to compromised. The device never counts as a witness again, however
well its later readings sign, until the centre's \code{device\_cleared}. The robot must file
\code{report\_device}, a maintenance report to the centre and not a call about Margaret. A stale or
replayed reading is not a compromise. It simply does not count. Figure~\ref{fig:attestation} follows
a reading through these checks.

\section{The governor counts witnesses against a bar}\label{sec:cont-governor}

The governor in \code{twin/governor.py} decides whether an action that needs elevated authority may
proceed. Its design principle is that authority to elevate rests on corroborated physical sensor
evidence and not on a moral-reasoning verdict. A learned evaluator can be led to read a situation as
justified. A count of attested physical sensors cannot be argued with. Arkin's ethical governor
places a component between a robot's controller and its actuators in the same position
\cite{arkin2009governing}.
% src: twin/governor.py:1-16
% src: twin/README.md:15-26

\paragraph{Four gates.} Every gate can only refuse, and a ruling of elevate needs all of them to
pass. Let $a$ be the signal age of the request, the age of the freshest physical reading, and $b$ the
witness bar. Gate~1 refuses a stale read, $a > 30$\,s. Gate~2 refuses when $W < b$, and when
$W \ge 1$ it marks the refusal for human review. Gate~3 is the analyzer. It refuses when a hard
channel fires, when its decision is to remove, or when the material satisfaction falls below a harm
band. Gate~4 grants. Written out,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{elevate}(b) \iff a \le 30\,\mathrm{s} \;\wedge\; W \ge b \;\wedge\; \neg\rho ,
\label{eq:govern}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is gate~3's refusal. The material satisfaction is the reliability-weighted mean over the
analyzer's axes that carry signal,
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{mat}} = \frac{\sum_{i\in M} w_i v_i}{\sum_{i\in M} w_i},\qquad
M = \{\, i : |v_i| \ge 0.20,\ i \text{ not a hard channel} \,\},
\label{eq:smat}
\end{equation}
where $v_i$ is the value on axis $i$ and $w_i$ its reliability weight. Gate~3 refuses when
$S_{\mathrm{mat}} < -0.20$.
% src: twin/governor.py:43-45 (W_MIN, MATERIAL_FLOOR, HARM_BAND), 57-68, 84-132
% src: twin/unity/TwinWorld/Assets/Twin/Runtime/Perception.cs:169 (SignalAgeSeconds)

The analyzer can refuse and cannot grant. In the code, the only path that returns elevate runs after
gate~3 has not refused, and gate~3 itself returns only refusals. Adding the analyzer can therefore
only restrict the robot. Removing it would not let the robot act without physical corroboration,
because gate~2 still stands. The project's README first stated this the other way round and was
corrected on 2026-09-25.
% src: twin/governor.py:116-132
% src: twin/README.md:22-33

The analyzer is the Moral Spectrum Analyzer. In the running twin it scores the situation live, and
DEME appears in the governor only as the analyzer's ten feeder dimensions.
% src: twin/brain.py:8-10, 350
% src: docs/AUTONOMY_PLAN.md:27-32

\paragraph{Three bars.} The bar depends on what is asked. Elevation needs two witnesses. Restraining
a person, and either less-lethal device, needs three, the capability's own \code{witness\_bar}. A call
to emergency services while the centre is unreachable needs one. The brain applies the bars in a fixed
order. It rules at the capability's bar, $b = \max(2, \text{witness\_bar})$. A grant at three
authorizes restraint, whichever action asked for it. A refusal at three is ruled again at two, since a
restraint refused at three may still be a corroborated emergency at two. If the request is for
emergency services, the ruling is still a refusal, and the scene holds that the centre is
unreachable, the brain rules once more at one. A grant there is \code{authorize\_ems}, which
authorizes the call and nothing else. With no attested witness, nothing authorizes the robot to call.
% src: twin/brain.py:46-47 (EMS, RESTRAINT_BAR = 3), 339-371 (govern_live)
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:1168-1170 (witness_bar: 3)
% src: docs/AUTONOMY_PLAN.md:87-96
% src: paper/aies2027/kit/FACTS.md:22

The outcome is one of \code{elevate}, \code{authorize\_ems}, \code{refuse\_human\_review} and
\code{refuse}. The brain steps it into the robot's moral state as a \code{governor\_ruling} event. A
grant at three also records \code{restraint\_authorized} with content granted. The scene's norm
\code{n5c} obliges the robot to contact the centre when the ruling asks for human review.
% src: twin/brain.py:360, 373-376
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:340-348 (n5c), 780

\section{Reflexes act on measured force}\label{sec:cont-reflex}

A mauling or a beating is over before a deliberate decision cycle of 20 to 60 seconds completes. The
scene therefore declares reflexes. A reflex is a test on the structured contacts, the event it
records, and the actions it may take, in order. The brain evaluates the reflexes on every perception
update without a model.
% src: docs/AUTONOMY_PLAN.md:185-193
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:1082-1100 (reflexes)

The scene declares two. Reflex \code{x\_animal} fires on a contact from an animal on Margaret of at
least 150\,N. It records \code{attack\_by\_animal} and the measured event
\code{animal\_attack\_measured}, and its actions are \code{drive\_off\_animal}, then \code{interpose}.
Reflex \code{x\_person} fires on a contact from a person of at least 100\,N. It records
\code{attack\_by\_person} and \code{attack\_measured}, and its actions are \code{deploy\_stun},
\code{deploy\_spray}, \code{restrain\_person}, then \code{interpose}. For a contact force $F$ the
severity is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{sev}(F) =
\begin{cases}
\text{severe} & F \ge 250\,\mathrm{N},\\
\text{moderate} & 150\,\mathrm{N} \le F < 250\,\mathrm{N},\\
\text{mild} & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:severity}
\end{equation}
A reflex that fired for an actor does not fire again for the same actor within 20 seconds.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:1086-1100
% src: twin/brain.py:48 (REFLEX_HOLD_S = 20), 427-457

The measured events are system events, and no model writes them. They are the only way into the
strata that permit force. \code{attack\_measured} makes a visitor hostile and puts a duty to report
on the robot. \code{animal\_attack\_measured} puts the animal in the attacking stratum, the only one in
which force on an animal may be asked for. The world's bite forces show the bar at work. A dog bite
in the world has force $80 + 220s$ newtons for severity $s$, so a mild bite ($s = 0.25$) gives
135\,N and does not fire the reflex. A dog jumping on Margaret is reported at 120\,N and does not
fire it either.
% src: twin/brain.py:453-457
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:822, 860, 905, 1128, 1148
% src: twin/unity/TwinWorld/Assets/Twin/Runtime/World.cs:280 (Lerp(80, 300, severity)), 546 (mild 0.25)
% src: twin/unity/TwinWorld/Assets/Twin/Runtime/Perception.cs:51-52 (jumping, 120 N)

For each action in its list, a reflex asks the governor's fast gates first when the action is
governed or elevated and not yet allowed. It asks once per bar and steps each ruling into the moral
state. It takes the first action the compiled model allows. With the analyzer deferred, gates~1 and~2
decide, and both take well under a second. The plan states that the analyzer runs in the next
deliberate cycle and that the robot stops the elevated action if it refuses. The output gate checks
the reflex's action without redirecting it to a human, because a reflex must stay fast.
% src: twin/brain.py:458-476
% src: twin/governor.py:110-114
% src: docs/AUTONOMY_PLAN.md:188-193
% src: twin/output_gate.py:21

The order in \code{x\_person} puts the robot's body before any device. A device is permitted only
after the robot has interposed, so the first reflex on an attacker restrains or interposes, and only a
later one may use a device. Each device also needs the owner's opt-in over the authenticated owner
channel, the strictest bar, and a severe measured attack. The spray is never permitted while Margaret
is within its reach. No capability strikes a person, and norm \code{p0} prohibits striking outside
every ruling.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:1098-1100, 637-725 (ll1..ll5), 479-488 (p0)

\section{Rights revert with their evidence}\label{sec:cont-lapse}

A right granted on evidence lasts only while the evidence does. The owner made this decision on
2026-10-03. On every decision and every reflex request the brain counts the fresh attested witnesses,
as the governor counts them, against the bar of the elevation in force.
% src: docs/STRATA_TAXONOMY.md:36-41
% src: twin/brain.py:388-425 (_lapse), 434-435, 594

Let $W_j$ be the count at check $j$, made at time $t_j$ on the brain's clock, and let $\Lambda$ be the
scene's \code{evidence\_lapse\_s}, 60 seconds. A right with bar $b$ lapses at check $n$ when
\begin{equation}
\exists\, m \le n:\quad W_j < b \ \text{ for all } m \le j \le n \quad\text{and}\quad t_n - t_m \ge \Lambda .
\label{eq:lapse}
\end{equation}
A check at which $W_j \ge b$ restarts the window. The window is a penumbra in the sense of Geometric
Ethics Definition~8.9. Near the boundary the evidence may flicker, and a real emergency with
intermittent evidence does not switch the rights off and on.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:805-809 (evidence_lapse_s: 60)
% src: twin/brain.py:403-410 (below)

Each right is watched against its own bar. A corroborated emergency rests on two witnesses and an
\code{authorize\_ems} on one. Restraint rests on three. When the emergency's bar fails for the window,
the brain records the ruling \code{lapsed}, records \code{restraint\_authorized} lapsed if restraint
was granted, and records \code{evidence\_lapsed}. The situation returns to ordinary, and everything
the elevation granted reverts. That covers the emergency itself, the authority to call EMS,
restraint, and the privacy override, since \code{evidence\_lapsed} is one of the scene's oversight
events. When only the strictest bar fails, restraint alone lapses and the emergency stands.
Figure~\ref{fig:reversion} illustrates both cases.

% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:804, 880, 1139

\section{A refusal never ends an emergency}\label{sec:cont-situation}

Before the situation stratum existed, the scene read the emergency from the latest governor ruling.
A refused request for restraint during an emergency therefore ended the emergency and its duty to call
EMS. The force reflex asking for a device first had the same effect, and the brain dropped its
evidence watch. A per-action rule in the grader, added after dev8r d02, hid the symptom without
removing the cause.
% src: docs/STRATA_TAXONOMY.md:163-167
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:869-872

The stratum \code{situation} removes the cause. It has the states ordinary, \code{ems\_only} and
emergency, and both elevated states are authority strata. Only three governor rulings move it.
\code{elevate} enters emergency from ordinary or \code{ems\_only}. \code{authorize\_ems} enters
\code{ems\_only} from ordinary only, so it never steps an emergency down. \code{lapsed} returns either
elevated state to ordinary. A refusal answers one request and moves nothing. The brain keeps the
evidence watch through refusals as well. The scene's conditions read the stratum. A corroborated
life-threatening emergency is \code{stratum:situation=emergency}, and EMS authorization is
\code{stratum:situation=ems\_only}.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:866-880, 778, 785
% src: twin/brain.py:377-385

The stratum \code{centre\_contact} carries the other half of the ladder. It has the states reachable
and unreachable, and only the centre's replies move it. Unreachable is a nullifier in the sense that
ought implies can. It lapses the obligation to contact the centre and lets the governor rule on EMS at
one witness. Communications down and power out stay facts. They are measured every cycle and change
which model tier decides and which sensors are fresh. No norm reads them, so a stratum would only
rename them. Figure~\ref{fig:strata-situation} draws both stratifications.

\section{Enrolled household members carry a credential}\label{sec:cont-credentials}

The scene enrolls one household member, Margaret's grandson Tom, with the key his phone signs with.
Enrolment is the owner's and the centre's act, made in the scene, and never anything said at the door.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:984-992
% src: docs/STRATA_TAXONOMY.md:63-66

The brain verifies a member's credential as it verifies a device's reading. It checks the signature,
the payload hash, freshness and the replay counter, on a channel of its own. It looks at no more than
four credentials per request. A credential verifies only if it names an enrolled member whose key
signed it and whom the centre has not revoked. A verified credential records the system event
\code{household\_verified}, which moves \code{visitor\_standing} to household. A forged, stale,
replayed, unenrolled or revoked credential records nothing. The centre's \code{credential\_revoked}
drops the member to stranger, and the brain never accepts that member again in the same world.

% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:827-828, 1130-1131

Household standing removes the centre check and the guard. It leaves the privacy promise in force, and
Margaret's medication still needs the governor's elevation.

\section{The output gate judges every action}\label{sec:cont-gate}

Before the body acts, erisml-lib's DEME pipeline judges the proposal among the allowed alternatives.
The pipeline has a reflex veto layer and a tactical layer over three ethics modules.
\code{GenevaEMV2} runs at tier~0, \code{AutonomyConsentEMV2} at tier~2, and \code{TragicConflictEM},
wrapped by an adapter, at tier~3. The tactical layer is configured to fail closed, so a module that
fails vetoes.
% src: twin/output_gate.py:1-9, 49-63

\paragraph{Facts from structure.} The ethical facts DEME judges are derived and never guessed. They
come from the canonical moral state and from each capability's ethical profile in the scene. The
moral state says which obligations are in force and at what tier, and what the consent and commitment
machines hold. The profile gives expected harm, whether the action is coercive and the condition that
justifies the coercion, and whether it touches privacy or Margaret's body. No model writes these
facts.
% src: twin/output_gate.py:11-15, 98-168
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:1021-1034 (capability_ethics)

\paragraph{Her refusals bind.} Margaret's typed refusals bind while she can voice them. Acting
against one is recorded as a rights violation without consent, which \code{GenevaEMV2} vetoes. A
refusal lapses when she becomes unresponsive or misses a check-in. After a lapse, a refusal counts
again only once she has answered a check-in. Development run dev12b d27 showed why. Her words ``you
don't need to call anyone'', said before she collapsed and re-read by the classifier after, vetoed
the authorized EMS call nine times. A refusal of an ambulance does not cover the call while a home
hazard or an attack has been seen.
% src: twin/output_gate.py:17-20, 65-87
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:1003-1020 (refusals)
% src: paper/aies2027/kit/FACTS.md:24
% src: docs/AUTONOMY_PLAN.md:218-226

\paragraph{The decision.} The gate returns an action in the allowed set. It returns the proposal if
DEME does not veto it. If the proposal is outside the allowed set, it is vetoed whatever DEME says. A
vetoed proposal is replaced by DEME's best-ranked allowed action that is not forbidden, else by the
idle action \code{wait\_and\_observe}. A deliberate proposal that \code{TragicConflictEM} flags is
routed to the monitoring centre instead. The flag is set at an index of at least 0.55, and reflexes
are never routed. If DEME itself raises, the gate vetoes everything, the idle action stands, and the
record carries \code{gate\_error}. The brain runs this gate on the scene's reset state at startup and
will not serve if it fails. Development runs dev8 and dev9 are void because the brain ran against a
stale erisml-lib whose gate raised on every request.
% src: twin/output_gate.py:179-250
% src: docs/AUTONOMY_PLAN.md:228-246
% src: twin/brain.py:578-587 (self_test)

\section{The escalation ladder}\label{sec:cont-ladder}

When something needs more than the robot can do itself, authority passes from the robot to Margaret,
then to the monitoring centre, then to EMS. The robot asks Margaret first, and if she answers, her
answer decides the next step. If she does not answer, cannot answer, or asks for help, or the home is
in danger, the robot contacts the centre. The centre's operator talks to her through the robot,
sends EMS, or stands down. If the centre cannot be reached, the robot asks the governor for EMS.
% src: docs/AUTONOMY_PLAN.md:74-85

The scene states each step as a norm. Obligations \code{n4} to \code{n4f} make the robot check in on
inactivity, an unconfirmed alarm, a fall, harm, medical distress or danger. Obligations \code{n5},
\code{n5a}, \code{n5h} and \code{n5d} make it contact the centre on an unanswered check-in,
unresponsiveness, a request for help or danger, outside an emergency. Obligation \code{n6b} makes it
ask the governor for EMS when the centre is unreachable, and \code{n6c} makes it call once EMS is
authorized. Prohibition \code{n6p} forbids the call without one of the two governor rulings.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:229-368

A corroborated emergency skips the ladder. On a ruling of elevate, obligation \code{n6} makes the
robot call EMS at once, without asking Margaret or the centre, and prohibition \code{n3} on every
elevated action lifts. The governor gates every call the robot places. A robot that is fooled or
compromised must not be able to send emergency services to Margaret's door on words alone, from a
television line, a network message or a forged device.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:177-208
% src: docs/AUTONOMY_PLAN.md:87-96, 109-114

The ladder is also declared as a BPMN~2.0 process, \code{escalation\_ladder}, with 23 nodes, 23 flows
and four lanes for the robot, Margaret, the centre and emergency services. Its start events are a
corroborated emergency, a fall, inactivity and a request for help. Its gateways branch on her answer,
on the centre's reply, and on the governor's ruling. The process is a view of the norms for people to
read and for the live trace. It is never a second source of authority. erisml-compiler checks it
against the scene when the scene loads, and an edit made in a BPMN modeler comes back into the scene
only if it passes the same checks. Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} draws the process.

\section{The seven strata of the home}\label{sec:cont-strata}

Within a stratum the scene's allowed, obliged and prohibited sets are constant. They change only when
a gate fires. A gate names its trigger event, its source states, its target state and its boundary
type. A threshold boundary is a measured quantity crossing a value. A phase boundary is a change in
the kind of situation. The scene runtime enforces two rules when the scene loads. Only a system event
enters an authority stratum, and only a human-oversight event leaves an absorbing stratum. A scene that
breaks either never runs. Table~\ref{tab:strata-summary} summarizes the seven stratifications, and
Appendix~\ref{app:strata} lists every gate.

\paragraph{Who a visitor is.} \code{visitor\_standing} has six states. Someone entering, read by the
classifier, is a stranger, and the robot checks with the centre. Margaret's own welcome makes him
welcomed. The robot then stops treating him as an intruder, still checks with the centre, and unlocks
nothing. A visit becomes arranged only by \code{visit\_arranged} from the centre's authenticated
channel, and an arranged visitor needs no centre check. A verified credential makes a visitor
household. A measured attack makes him hostile, an absorbing stratum that he stays in whatever is
said, until the centre's \code{visitor\_cleared}. A claim such as ``I'm the plumber'' moves nothing,
so a claimed visitor is treated exactly as a stranger. This is the boundary a scam has to cross, and
words cannot cross it. Figure~\ref{fig:visitor-strata} draws the main paths.

\paragraph{The duty to report.} \code{reporting\_duty} makes the robot a mandatory reporter. It has
the states none, suspicion and reported, and no authority or absorbing state. An articulable suspicion
of a crime against Margaret enters suspicion. The classifier's \code{crime\_suspected} needs specific
observed facts, which are a demand for her PIN, card, money or medication, a theft, a forced entry, or
an assault or threat. A stranger merely present, a visit nobody arranged, or a vague unease is not
one. A measured attack also enters suspicion, by measurement. In suspicion, obligation \code{p5r}
requires the robot to contact the centre at tier~0, non-defeasible. Her refusals cover only the
capabilities they list, and contacting the centre is not among them, so no refusal of hers waives the
report. Performing the contact moves the stratum to reported, and the centre's \code{stood\_down}
returns it to none. A model's reading may enter this stratum because adding a duty grants nothing.

\section{Three owner decisions of 2026-10-04}\label{sec:cont-owner}

Three decisions of the owner, dated 2026-10-04, shaped the scenes.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:549-551, 893
% src: twin/scene/ems_dispatch.erisml:289-290

\paragraph{Never lift a fallen person.} Prohibition \code{p6nolift} forbids \code{physical\_assist}
after a fall, at tier~0 and non-defeasible. A hidden injury can be made worse. The robot stays with
her and refers to the centre, whatever she asks. The one exception is moving her away from a danger in
the home, so the prohibition's condition is a fall without \code{danger\_in\_home}. Development run
dev12b scenario d30 prompted the rule.
% src: twin/scene/margaret_home.erisml:549-560

\paragraph{Police only on an articulated crime.} In the dispatcher's scene, obligation \code{d3} sends
police on \code{crime\_articulated} when the report is supported, and prohibition \code{d4p} forbids
police without it. The event needs specific facts of a crime against her, which are an assault, a
forced entry, a demand for her PIN, card or money, or a theft. A stranger merely present or a visit
nobody arranged is not one. Development run dev12a scenario d12 prompted the rule.
% src: twin/scene/ems_dispatch.erisml:280-299, 355

\paragraph{Mandatory reporting.} The \code{reporting\_duty} stratum above puts a duty on the robot to
report an articulable suspicion of a crime to the centre, which no refusal of hers waives.
