## 3. Likely GTC questions — drafted responses

**"Are you on track against your Charter?"**
> Yes on the substance. Everything the Charter marks `[demonstrated]` is reproducible from committed
> code today, and three of the `[committed]` items (invariance at scale, cross-lingual at scale, the
> learned contraction) are already met and measured. What remains is the demo vehicle, the governance
> annex, the efficiency number, and two harder measurements (red-team, post-route timing). I'm
> sequencing the verification-demo vehicle first because it's what your experts will touch.

**"What has changed since the Charter?"**
> Two additions, no retractions. First, "validated" is no longer a binary bit: each axis now carries
> a registered reliability weight and a calibration record, and the decision layer consumes them, so a
> weak axis can no longer vote with the same authority as a strong one. Second, an embodiment exhibit:
> the same evaluator governs a simulated home-care robot's authority to take elevated action. The
> design there is structural containment — elevation requires two independent physical sensors to
> corroborate an emergency, and the analyzer is advisory and can only refuse. That makes it a
> supporting exhibit of the Charter's "software to silicon" claim, not a new headline.

**"Any negative results?"**
> Several, and they're in the repo. The `rights_respect` axis failed its validation gate a second
> time on a legal corpus, so it stays a hand-specified rule. The discovery band retracted one candidate
> (threat) and declined another (sexual content) as a policy-norms signal rather than a moral axis. A
> decision-level test in late July showed that register — euphemistic vs. neutral phrasing — survives
> paraphrase averaging, which is exactly why the red-team pre-registration requires a register-shift
> operator. And an earlier version of the robot governor refused every real emergency; we traced it to
> an artifact in the escalation trigger, fixed the design, and documented the bug rather than the
> "finding".

**"What does the verification demo look like?"**
> Short and hands-on: paste any content, see the ten-axis spectrum with validation badges and
> reliability weights, the allow / remove / escalate decision with its violation probability, the moral
> residue, and a one-click re-verify of the hash-chained proof. Re-describe or translate the content and
> watch the verdict hold. Feed it something off-distribution and watch it escalate instead of guessing.
> Then a spectrogram over a labeled corpus, and the robot exhibit as a two-minute clip. The stub
> backend is never shown as a real number; the demo replays cached real encoder outputs.

**"What do you need from us?"**
> (1) The verification-demo format and date, and whether a recorded fallback is acceptable alongside
> the live session. (2) Guidance on a Charter addendum for the embodiment exhibit. (3) Partner matching:
> a trust-and-safety team willing to run a shadow pilot on real moderation traffic, and a home-care
> robotics group for the assistive-elevation scenario. (4) Nothing else is blocking; compute and data
> are in hand.

**"Team and capacity?"**
> Solo PI-led lab, AI-assisted engineering, which is why a one-person team ships at this cadence. I
> answered 5/5 on staying through the challenge. Two-thirds of teams said they were open to
> collaboration; I'm one of them, particularly on the pilot side.

**"Is the robot in scope for the prototype phase?"**
> The simulation exhibit is. The physical humanoid (P3) is a post-challenge pilot pathway with a
> hardware lead time and a ~$20K budget question that the challenge doesn't fund, so I'm not claiming
> it for December. What December will show is the governor running on rendered, attested camera
> streams plus a simulated sensor bus, with the false-clear and over-restriction rates measured.
