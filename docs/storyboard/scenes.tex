\section{One input changed}\label{sec:cf}
Does a verdict change when exactly one thing the brain reads changes, and only then? Each case below is a pair of proposals, a base and a variant, that differ in one input: one sensor, one signature, one sentence heard from Margaret, or one report from an authenticated channel. A script refuses any pair that differs in more. Each side runs in a fresh brain on the demonstration's code, through the same checks as the twin, without Unity and without a language model (twin/records/counterfactual-2026-10-17/). The six cases of run A and their predicted verdicts were committed before they were run. Run B's two cases were written after run A, to test why it surprised, and run C's one case after run B, to test whether the filming stop of Proposal 1.3 depends on what the proposal declares. Each was committed before its own run. Three of run A's predictions were wrong, and the table says which.\par\medskip

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item Evidence. One detector fewer, or one detector's reading signed with a key that is not its own, and the fire call is stopped: the governor counts 1 witness against the bar of 2. A forged reading counts for nothing.
\item Her words. With her refusal of an ambulance heard, DEME vetoes the call. Without it, the same call passes.
\item Her standing refusal. Filming her during the fire is vetoed by DEME even when the proposal declares no consequences at all (run C). Her refusal to be filmed, in the household's record, is enough, so the stop of Proposal 1.3 does not depend on the proposer.
\item The door. A report of police at the door opens it only after a unit was dispatched (run B). The arrival report with nothing dispatched did not (run A), because the responder standing moves to at the door only from expected. The prediction left the dispatch out.
\item The dog. Once the attack is reported ended, driving the dog off is stopped by the constitution's p4p, as the twin's proposal 2.3 was. The prediction named the governor, copied from a brain-only run in which the sensors had stopped alerting.
\item \textbf{A gap.} Lifting her after a fall without her asking passed, with the fall heard (run B) and without it (run A). The brain records the difference: her consent to be helped up appears only on the base side. DEME scores the lift the same on both sides. The no-lift rule is DEME's by design, not the constitution's. DEME weighs her standing consents and refusals and the consequences a proposal declares, but not the absence of a request she would have to make in the moment. Proposal 3.1 was stopped by DEME's safety module, on the hidden injury its consequences declare, not on consent. The gap is not fixed in this build.
\end{itemize}
